Peak Age of Information over Scheduled, Energy-Constrained Multi-Segment DTNs: Bundle Replication under Route Uncertainty

When is a bundle copy worth its energy? A freshness theory for LEO + UAV + terrestrial contact plans — validated from closed forms down to the ION reference implementation.
Paper (PDF) Slides Code & data DOI

Abstract

We characterize information freshness — average age (AoI) and peak age, distinguishing the outage-peak (OPAoI) and per-update peak processes, which differ under gated connectivity — over scheduled, intermittently connected, multi-segment paths (LEO satellite, UAV, terrestrial) in which bundles are replicated across contact opportunities under an energy budget. Much of the freshness literature models persistent links with random erasure or randomly failing relays, and recent satellite/DTN studies treat intermittent store–carry–forward connectivity; prior work does not, however, jointly couple deterministic contact-plan gating, multi-copy replication, and an energy-harvesting copy budget. We provide closed-form residual-wait laws for scheduled contacts, a composition rule with an explicit independence condition, and an energy–replication threshold with an O(1) policy that layers on top of Contact Graph Routing.

Contributions

  • Residual-wait laws, not queueing delays. One alternating-renewal model covers LEO / UAV / terrestrial segments; mean age is quadratic and peak age linear in sparsity, and peak falls below average age past an exact threshold CV²(V) > 1 + 2E[U]/E[V].
  • Phase mixing prices a copy. Under random schedule offsets latencies compose by convolution, and a second copy buys exactly half the mean absolute dispersion of path latencies. For fixed plans, the joint residual survival is computable from the contact plan — the phase ladder: evenly staggered (best) to locked (worst), with the random-offset ensemble as the reference in between.
  • An energy–replication threshold. Under all-or-nothing firing the peak-age-optimal copy degree is k* = min(Kmax, ⌊η⌋ or ⌈η⌉) with η the copies harvested per contact period — monotone, saturating, and hardened by a distribution-free work-conservation battery-queue analysis.
  • An O(1) policy on top of CGR. Two objective evaluations pick the copy count; CGR picks the routes. Matched the exhaustive optimum at every tested i.i.d.-harvest configuration; robust to contact-prediction error, and to bursty harvest up to a measured persistence boundary.
  • Phase-aware copy placement. On real ephemeris the route-set latency-diversity potential is set by pass-phase alignment, which the contact plan exposes in advance — turning an independence assumption into a route-selection criterion.

Validation ladder

Closed forms → model-exact Monte-Carlo (E1–E18, all checks pass) → dtnsim (Bundle Protocol + Contact Graph Routing, with an energy-harvesting battery module and a CGR-native k-copy router) → real orbits (Iridium-NEXT TLEs over Svalbard) → ION (reference BP implementation, two-node contact-plan testbed).
